Brand color · Linex Loyalty

What color is Loyalty? Four candidates, one umbrella.

Linex Loyalty is the brand that sits above all the others — Travel, Go, Trip, Marketplace and Rewards all live inside it. It doesn't have its own color or a finished logo yet. This page compares four candidates on real pieces of a points program, so leadership can choose with data.

i

About the logo: Loyalty doesn't have a custom logo yet (we checked the brand folder and the Linex Go and UltraGroup manuals). What you see below is a proposed wordmark, using the same typographic formula already used by Linex Trip and Linex Go: "Linex" + the brand's word in the accent color. It's here to compare color, not to approve a final logo.

01 · Brand color theory

What each color says, before we even look at Linex

Every color already carries a meaning out in the world, regardless of who inside the company uses it. Here's what each one says on its own, and why it does — or doesn't — fit a rewards brand.

Green #3CCEAB
CoolFreshMedium saturation

Communicates: growth, a rising balance, a healthy state.

This is the mint-green that already dominates fintech — savings, investing, "your money growing." That metaphor works fine for points that also grow, but it's a meaning borrowed from finance apps, not something native to rewards.

Magenta #CC3383
WarmVibrantHigh saturation

Communicates: generosity, a surprise, a perk you didn't have to ask for.

This is the color behind the category's biggest precedent for exactly this job: T-Mobile built "Tuesdays" — its perks program — entirely in magenta, distinct from the carrier's own color, to separate "the perk" from "the service." It doesn't borrow meaning from another category, and it doesn't resemble anything else in the Linex family.

Red #CC333F
WarmHigh impactHigh saturation

Communicates: urgency, energy, an immediate decision.

This is the heaviest color of the four visually — great for a "redeem now" button, harder to sustain as a permanent brand color: in almost any product system, red is already reserved for alerts and errors. A points balance shown in red can read as "something's wrong" before it reads as a benefit earned.

Gold #C99A3B
WarmEarthyMedium saturation

Communicates: achievement, status, something earned — not given.

It comes straight out of the category's own visual language: medals, Bronze/Silver/Gold tiers, premium cards. It doesn't compete for attention the way red does, and it doesn't borrow meaning from another category the way green does — it already means "reward" before it says a word.

03 · Legibility

Can you read “12,480 points” in this color?

A pretty brand color that can't be read isn't much use — and in a points program, a balance is the first thing anyone looks for.

04 · Market landscape

What color do other points programs use?

Loyalty competes for perception with loyalty programs, not with OTAs — the default color here is a different one.

Program / brandColorHexTakeaway
Loylogic · global rewards marketplaceRed#E6003CThe world's largest rewards marketplace is already red
iTravy · luxury travel on pointsGold/brass#C8A24ENearly identical to our Gold candidate, on an almost-black background
Xoxoday · AI-powered rewards, loyalty & incentivesOrangeorange (approx.)Close to Linex Go's coral, but a different lane: corporate incentives, not traveler loyalty
Tremendous · gift card & rewards platform for businessesBlue#006FF7Yet another blue in the category — none of our four candidates repeat that already-saturated territory

iTravy — the luxury-travel-on-points platform closest to what Loyalty could be — already uses almost the exact gold we're proposing, on a near-black background: it validates that reading directly. Loylogic, the world's largest rewards marketplace, is red: it validates Red as a real option for the category, not just ours. Xoxoday is orange — close to Linex Go's coral, but competing in a different lane (corporate incentives, not traveler loyalty). Tremendous is blue, like much of the payments and gift-card space — one more reason not to repeat that color for Loyalty. Purple stays off the table: both LATAM Pass and UltraGroup already use it, which is why we didn't include it as a candidate.

05 · Takeaway

Our recommendation

Design verdict

Loyalty is the parent brand: it defines its color first, the family adjusts after.

All four candidates are valid options. We evaluated them on their own merit — real legibility, and how well they fit what Loyalty wants to represent — not on whether they happen to match a sibling today. If the chosen color ends up shared with Trip or Go, those brands update afterward; the right order is for the umbrella to decide first.

  • 01
    Green reads very well on navy and is a natural pick if Loyalty and Trip want to share a visual family from day one.
  • 02
    Magenta is the most well-rounded of the four: it passes the legibility test on white (something only Red also does), doesn't repeat any color in the Linex family, and has a strong precedent in the category — it's the same move T-Mobile uses to separate its perks ("Tuesdays") from its service brand.
  • 03
    Red, along with Magenta, is the most legible of the four on white (passes AA without needing a darker variant). Worth noting: in interfaces it tends to read as alert or urgency before it reads as identity — best reserved for elements where that won't create noise.
  • 04
    Gold (our proposal): it's the color the loyalty category itself already uses worldwide to say "tier earned, benefit, points," and no member of the Linex family uses it today. It's weaker on pure white (it needs its Bronze for text), but it's the option most specific to what Loyalty represents as a category.
